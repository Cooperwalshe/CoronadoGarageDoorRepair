My door won't open and there was a loud bang last night. What happened?

Almost certainly a broken torsion spring. That bang is the spring letting go, and afterwards the door feels enormously heavy because the springs were carrying nearly all its weight. Don't run the opener — it isn't built to lift the door on its own and you'll damage the motor or bend panels. Describe it in the form below and we'll get someone out.

How much does a spring replacement cost?

It depends on the door's weight, whether it uses one spring or two, and the cycle rating you choose. Anyone quoting a firm price before seeing the door is guessing. What we can tell you is that the technician will diagnose it, give you a number, and wait for your go-ahead before doing the work.

Why do garage doors fail faster in Coronado?

Salt air. The hardware — springs, cables, hinges, roller bearings — is mostly bare or lightly coated steel, and constant salt exposure corrodes it well ahead of schedule. A spring rated for 10,000 cycles can fall short once rust gets into the coils. Properties in the Cays and along the Strand see this worst.

Can I replace a torsion spring myself?

No. A wound torsion spring stores enough energy to break bones or worse, and it releases in a fraction of a second if it's handled wrong. This is the one repair where the DIY route genuinely isn't worth it. Extension springs on older doors are also under serious tension and carry the same warning.

My door goes down a foot and comes back up. Why?

Usually the safety sensors. Two small units sit near the floor on either side of the opening and have to see each other. A bike, a box, or a knock that pushed one out of alignment will break the beam. Check for obstructions and look at the indicator lights — if one is dark or flickering, they're misaligned. If the sensors are clear and it still reverses, the opener's force or limit settings likely need adjusting.

How long does a typical repair take?

Most common repairs — springs, cables, rollers, sensor alignment — are done in a single visit of one to two hours. Opener replacement is similar. A new door install takes longer and usually needs the door ordered first.

Can I get someone out today?

Often, yes. Coronado is small, so travel time between visits is short. Doors stuck open, doors that won't close, and cars trapped inside get priority — flag that in the form if you need someone today.

Should I repair my opener or replace it?

Under about ten years old with a single failed part, repair usually makes sense. Older units with several problems tend to cost more in repeat visits than a replacement would. Anything made before 1993 lacks modern safety reversal and should be replaced regardless of condition.

Is it worth paying more for coastal-rated hardware?

On this island, generally yes. Galvanized or powder-coated hardware, corrosion-resistant springs, and sealed-bearing nylon rollers cost more up front and last meaningfully longer in salt air. If you're replacing components anyway, it's the right moment to ask about the coastal spec.

Do you work on base housing at NAS North Island?

Work at base housing may need to go through housing maintenance rather than an outside contractor. Check with your housing office first — it can save you the cost of a visit.
